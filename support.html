    Support BareBoard

    Our mission

    BareBoard exists so that anyone, anywhere, can learn how the hardware in their life actually works — for free, forever.

    They seal the schematics. They hide the datasheets behind NDAs. They glue the batteries in, serialize the parts, and tell you that opening your own device voids your right to understand it. We open it anyway — legally, carefully, and in public.

    Every device on this bench gets the full treatment: chip-level identification, boot-log forensics, board mapping, protocol analysis, and honest documentation of what the manufacturer won’t tell you. UART headers, JTAG pads, hidden radios, unsigned bootloaders, silent data exfiltration — if it’s on the board, it goes in the report.

    This is for the hardware hackers, the repair techs, the reverse engineers, the curious students desoldering their first board at 2 AM. For everyone who believes that if you bought it, you own it — and owning it means understanding it.

    No paywall. No ads. No sponsored findings. No selling your data. No venture capital deciding what gets published. Just the truth about the circuit boards, published openly under the principle that technical literacy is a public good — and the right to understand your own hardware shouldn’t depend on your income, your credentials, or your geography.

    That’s the mission. Your support is what keeps the bench powered.

    Why your support matters

    Free to read doesn’t mean free to run. Every teardown starts with buying the device — often outbidding e-waste scrappers for the interesting ones — then opening it with real tools, probing it with real instruments, and spending the hours to turn raw silicon into knowledge worth publishing.

    A single thorough autopsy can mean a logic analyzer, a hot-air station, replacement probes, and a week of bench time. The rare boards — the ones nobody else has documented — cost the most to rescue. Your donation doesn’t buy coffee. It buys the next device that gets opened for everyone.

    There’s no venture capital, no corporate sponsor, no premium tier coming later. BareBoard is funded the old-fashioned way: by the people who read it. If this site ever taught you something, saved you a repair bill, or just fed your curiosity — feed the parts jar and keep it running for the next hacker.

    Where every dollar goes

    • Bench devices. Teardowns need hardware — the rarer the device, the more it costs to rescue one from a landfill or an auction listing and get it onto the bench.
    • Test equipment. Logic analyzers, UART adapters, soldering gear, microscopes — the instruments that turn a pile of chips into documented knowledge.
    • Domain & hosting. The unglamorous cost of staying online and staying independent.

    A donation buys no influence. Findings are published on the evidence — never on who’s paying. No sponsored teardowns, no embargoed results, no pay-to-play coverage. Ever.

    Other ways to help

    Money isn’t the only currency here:

    • Spread the word. Send one teardown to one curious person. That does as much as a donation.
    • Donate hardware. Got a dead or retired device gathering dust? It might be our next autopsy. (Contact details on the About page.)
    • Correct us. Found an error in a report? Tell us. Accuracy is the whole product.
    • Send us a target. Know a device with undocumented hardware, a locked bootloader begging for analysis, or a board nobody’s mapped? Point us at it.
    • Link us. Writing about hardware? Cite our findings. Open knowledge grows by being referenced.

    A note from the bench

    We started BareBoard on a simple belief: education should be a human right — free and free to access. Everything here will always be free to read. No paywall, no premium tier, no exceptions. Thank you for keeping it that way.

    BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

    • No AI training: All text, findings, and original photography on this site are copyrighted. Automated scraping for AI model training is prohibited. Crawlers that ignore robots.txt are trespassing.
    • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
    • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
    • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
    • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.
