A note from the bench

We started BareBoard on a simple belief: education should be a human right — free and free to access. Everything here will always be free to read. No paywall, no premium tier, no exceptions.

Free to read doesn’t mean free to run. The bench needs devices to tear down, probes and adapters to measure them with, and a domain to live on. If this site ever taught you something, chip in and keep it running for the next person.

Chip in

Everything here is free and stays free — that’s the whole point. But free to read isn’t free to run: the bench needs devices to open, probes to measure them with, and a domain to live on.

If BareBoard ever taught you something, saved you a repair bill, or just fed your curiosity — you can feed the parts jar. Any amount, one-time or monthly, entirely your call. And if money’s tight, sending the site to one curious person does just as much.

Where it goes

  • Bench devices. Teardowns need hardware — the rarer the device, the more it costs to get one onto the bench.
  • Test equipment. Probes, adapters, and bench gear that make the findings possible.

About

BareBoard Legal Framework & Declarations

  • Educational nature: BareBoard operates exclusively as a non-commercial, open educational resource. All material is compiled for academic evaluation, hardware preservation, and technical literacy under 17 U.S.C. § 107 (fair use).
  • Zero-leak / no-binary clause: This site does not host, distribute, or mirror proprietary firmware binaries, encrypted operating system keys, or weaponizable exploits. Documentation covers only the public, physical realities of circuit hardware.
  • Property verification: Every hardware autopsy is performed on personally owned, legally acquired equipment. No rented or third-party infrastructure is used.
  • Public source sourcing: Comparative and regulatory data is aggregated from official public filings, including the U.S. Federal Communications Commission equipment authorization database.

BareBoard — open hardware allies, documented in alignment with the Open Source Hardware Association framework. Text and original photography published under open terms; nothing here is for sale.
