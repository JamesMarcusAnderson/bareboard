The Chromecast with Google TV (4K, 2020)

The Chromecast grew up: Google TV, a real remote, and an Amlogic brain — with every chip ID confirmed by a comparative teardown. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The Chromecast with Google TV (4K) is Google’s 2020 streaming dongle and the first Chromecast with an on-screen interface you navigate yourself: Google TV on Android TV, with a bundled Bluetooth voice remote. Google released it on September 30, 2020 at $49.99.Wikipedia AFTVnews

EDN’s comparative teardown of the 4K and the later HD editions confirmed the 4K board’s chips: an Amlogic S905D3 SoC, 2 GB of SK hynix LPDDR4, 8 GB of Samsung eMMC, and a BCM43598 wireless chip.EDN

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The outside

An oval pebble in Snow — also sold in Sky and Sunrise — with an attached HDMI tail, USB-C power, and a small oval remote with dedicated YouTube and Netflix buttons.Wikipedia

The board

EDN’s teardown found a two-heatsink sandwich with thermal paste on both sides, a screw-mounted HDMI cable, and PCB-embedded antennas flanking the wireless chip.EDN EDN

The FCC filing

FCC ID A4RGZRNL, Google LLC; grant dated August 23, 2020 (application August 2, 2020), described as an “Interactive Media Streaming Device.” Test data: Bluetooth 0.0084 W; 5 GHz entries 0.0479–0.049 W across 20/40/80 MHz; 2.4 GHz at 0.0046 W and WLAN at 0.0589 W.FCC filing

Service and debug modes

The voice remote pairs over Bluetooth during Google Home setup; the remote is bundled with the device per Google’s own product materials.Wikipedia We found no publicly documented serial console, JTAG, or SWD interface for this unit — we listen, we don’t knock.

What the public record tells us

  • The HD edition proved the 4K board. EDN’s side-by-side teardown of the 4K and HD editions confirmed every 4K chip ID by comparison — the rare case where a cheaper sibling sharpens the picture of the flagship.EDN

By the numbers

  • FCC ID: A4RGZRNL — grant 2020-08-23
  • Main chip: Amlogic S905D3 (quad Cortex-A55, Mali-G31 MP2)
  • Memory / storage: 2 GB LPDDR4 / 8 GB eMMC
  • Wireless: BCM43598 (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth 5.1)
  • Video: 4K (HD edition: 1080p, Amlogic S805X2)
  • Power: USB-C
  • Launch price: $49.99

Words to know

PCB-embedded antenna
An antenna drawn as copper traces right on the circuit board — no separate part, which is why EDN could spot them flanking the wireless chip.
Google TV vs Android TV
Android TV is the operating system; Google TV is the interface layered on top — the home screen you actually navigate.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
