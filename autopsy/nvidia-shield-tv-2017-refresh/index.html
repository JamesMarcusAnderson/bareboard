The NVIDIA Shield TV (2017)

NVIDIA’s gaming streamer, second act: the same Tegra X1 in a smaller, remote-first box — and don’t confuse it with the 2019 tube. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The 2017 Shield TV (model P2897, codename “darcy”) is NVIDIA’s refreshed Android TV streamer: a Tegra X1, 3 GB of RAM, and 16 GB of storage in a smaller angular chassis, shown at CES in January 2017. It does 4K HDR, with Gigabit Ethernet, dual USB 3.0, and 2×2 Wi-Fi.Droid Life 9to5Google

This is the darcy generation — not the 2019 “mdarcy” refresh with its tube-shaped redesign. NVIDIA’s own 2019 FCC permissive change swapped the wireless chip to a CYW4356 in later-built units, so the radio depends on when your unit was made.9to5Google

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The FCC filing

FCC ID VOB-P2897, NVIDIA Corporation, grant dated August 29, 2016, model P2897. A 2019 permissive change to the filing replaced the original BCM4354/CYW4354 wireless with a CYW4356 in later-built units — same model number, different radio by build date.FCC filing 9to5Google

Service and debug modes

The controller pairs wirelessly; the box exposes two USB 3.0 ports and Ethernet for storage and networking.HotHardware We found no publicly documented UART, JTAG, or SWD interface for this unit — we listen, we don’t knock.

What the public record tells us

  • Two radios, one model number. The 2019 permissive change means “Shield TV 2017” units can carry different wireless chips depending on when they were built.9to5Google
  • No microSD on the refresh. The 2017 box dropped the card slot; USB storage took its place.HotHardware

Words to know

Permissive change
An FCC procedure that lets a manufacturer swap a component — here, the wireless chip — in an already-approved product without starting the filing over.
Codename (darcy / mdarcy)
Internal project names. “Darcy” is the 2017 refresh; “mdarcy” is the distinct 2019 hardware — different device, different report.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
