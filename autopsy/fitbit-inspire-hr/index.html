The Autopsy Report · Device #056

Fitbit Inspire HR

Fitbit's entry-level heart-rate tracker is everywhere — and inside, it's a mystery. No public teardown names its chips. This report says that plainly.

What is this thing?

The Fitbit Inspire HR is an entry-level fitness wristband: continuous heart-rate monitoring, activity and sleep tracking, and a small touchscreen. Our research slice records model FB413 with FCC ID XRAFB413 — but the FCC filing itself was unreachable in this pass (the site returned an error), so treat the FCC details as unverified until we can read them directly. What's verifiable now comes from the user manual: the tracker is rated water-resistant to 50 meters and shows its regulatory information under Settings → Device Info. User manual

Not yet on our bench

Straightforward honesty first: BareBoard has not possessed, disassembled, tested, booted, or touched the firmware of this unit. We don't have one on the bench yet. Everything below is built from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer material, datasheets, manuals, and published teardowns — with every claim linked to its source and graded for confidence. When a fact genuinely requires our hands on hardware (boot logs, our own photos, exact board revision of a future unit), we say so out loud.

That framing is what makes publishing now legitimate: this is a public-records autopsy, and it will grow into a bench autopsy the day a unit lands on our desk.

The outside

A slim tracker module clipped into a soft wristband. The front is a small touchscreen; the back carries the optical heart-rate sensor windows — the red-sensor area users occasionally report condensation inside. The tracker detaches for charging on its dedicated cradle. iFixit Answers

The board

Honestly: we don't know. No public teardown identifies the Inspire HR's board or its chips, and the FCC filing's internal-photo exhibit hasn't been verified from a reachable source. We won't borrow parts from the Inspire 2, the Charge family, or any other Fitbit — similar names are not evidence. This section stays empty until the bench or a genuine public teardown fills it.

Debug & service modes

Our posture: we listen, we don't knock. Only publicly verified interfaces.

User-facing (from the manual). The Inspire HR can be restarted, updated, and erased through its on-device settings and the Fitbit app. Regulatory information is displayed on-device under Settings → Device Info. User manual

No public debug interface. No serial console, JTAG header, or diagnostic mode appears in any public record we found.
