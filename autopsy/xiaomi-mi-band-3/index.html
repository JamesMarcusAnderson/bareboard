The Autopsy Report · Device #051

The Xiaomi Mi Band 3 (2018)

A fitness band with a 20-day battery, an OLED touchscreen, and a Dialog Bluetooth chip — plus a quiet hardware revision split between China and the rest of the world. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The Mi Band 3 is Xiaomi’s third-generation fitness band, released May 31, 2018: a 0.78-inch 128×80 OLED touchscreen, Bluetooth 4.2 BLE, an optical heart-rate monitor, an accelerometer, and 5 ATM water resistance — with a 110 mAh battery rated for 20 days.Wikipedia

The Bluetooth radio is a Dialog DA14681, per a teardown comparison of the global and Chinese versions — which also found the two regions shipped different hardware revisions (v0.18.4.1 vs v0.18.3.2) with different populated parts on similar layouts.XDA Forums

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The board

The FCC exhibit’s board photos are public. The teardown comparison identifies the Dialog DA14681 Bluetooth SoC; the heart-rate sensor and accelerometer part numbers are not publicly identified, and the two regional revisions populate different parts on similar layouts.XDA Forums

Service and debug modes

The capacitive button and tap gestures are the documented interface; a pre-release manual leak noted the clip-on charging cable was unchanged from the Mi Band 2.TelecomTalk We found no publicly documented UART, JTAG, or SWD interface — we listen, we don’t knock.

What the public record tells us

  • Two bands, two boards. The global and Chinese versions are different hardware revisions with different populated parts — same silhouette, different guts.XDA Forums
  • The whole computer is one chip. A Dialog BLE SoC runs the band — Bluetooth, MCU, and all.Datasheet (PDF)

Words to know

5 ATM
Water-resistance rating: survives pressure equivalent to 50 meters of still water. Showers and swims, not scuba.
BLE
Bluetooth Low Energy — the sipping-not-gulping flavor of Bluetooth that lets a band run for weeks on 110 mAh.
Hardware revision
A version stamp on the board (here v0.18.4.1 vs v0.18.3.2) — different revisions can carry different parts.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
