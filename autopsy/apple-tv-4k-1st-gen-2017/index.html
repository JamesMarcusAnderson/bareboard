The Apple TV 4K (2017)

Apple’s first 4K box: an iPad Pro chip in a black puck, a new fan to keep it cool, and the last Apple TV that was easy to take apart. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The Apple TV 4K (first generation) is Apple’s 2017 streaming box: 4K video with HDR, an Apple A10X Fusion chip borrowed from the iPad Pro, 3 GB of memory, and Gigabit Ethernet. iFixit’s teardown, published September 26, 2017, found a new cooling system — a fan venting through eight openings in the bottom — built to tame the A10X.MacRumors AppleInsider

It’s also the model where Apple removed the USB-C diagnostic port found on its predecessor — and iFixit still gave it an 8-out-of-10 repairability score.iClarified MacRumors

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

A short history

  1. September 12, 2017FCC grant for BCGA1842 (“Apple TV 4k”), Apple Inc.FCC filing
  2. September 26, 2017iFixit publishes its teardown: A10X Fusion, 3 GB LPDDR4 (2 + 1 GB SK hynix dies), 32 GB Toshiba flash, Murata wireless module, fan cooling, and no USB-C port.MacRumors

The outside

A black puck in the same family shape as the Apple TV HD, paired with the black Siri Remote with its glass touch surface. The teardown photos show the familiar top with the Apple logo and “tv” mark, and the new vented bottom.iClarified

The board

iFixit’s board read is the backbone here. The A10X Fusion sits under a heatsink with a fan behind it, exhausting through eight openings in the bottom case — the first Apple TV that needed active cooling. Memory is 3 GB of SK hynix LPDDR4 split across two parts (2 GB + 1 GB); storage is 32 GB of Toshiba NAND. Wireless runs through a Murata 339S00381 module reported as a Broadcom BCM43572, video output goes through a MegaChips DisplayPort-to-HDMI converter, and wired networking is full Gigabit Ethernet. The USB-C port of the previous generation is gone.MacRumors AppleInsider

The FCC filing

FCC ID BCGA1842, Apple Inc., grant dated September 12, 2017, listed as “Apple TV 4k.” Test data: Bluetooth 0.11 W, 2.4 GHz WLAN up to 0.191 W, and 5 GHz WLAN up to 0.24 W.FCC filing

Service and debug modes

The USB-C diagnostic port is gone on this generation — the teardown confirms its removal. We found no publicly documented serial console, JTAG, or SWD interface for this unit — we listen, we don’t knock.

What the public record tells us

  • Cooling got serious. The A10X’s heat demanded a fan and eight bottom vents — the first Apple TV with active cooling, and the teardown that proved it.AppleInsider
  • A port vanished. The USB-C diagnostic port from the previous generation was removed, yet repairability stayed high at 8/10.MacRumors

Words to know

DisplayPort-to-HDMI converter
A bridge chip that translates one video-output language into another — here letting the A10X’s DisplayPort talk to an HDMI TV.
Repairability score
iFixit’s 1–10 rating of how easy a device is to take apart and fix. 8 is excellent for a sealed puck.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
