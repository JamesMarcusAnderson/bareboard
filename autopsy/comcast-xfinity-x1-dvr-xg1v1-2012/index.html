The Autopsy Report · Device #045

The Comcast XG1v1 (Pace PX001ANM)

Comcast’s first X1 DVR: six tuners, a CableCARD, and a 500 GB hard drive — with its schematics locked behind permanent FCC confidentiality. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The XG1v1 — built by Pace as the PX001ANM — is the set-top box Comcast shipped for its X1 platform: a high-definition DVR with six QAM tuners, DOCSIS 3.0 networking, a CableCARD slot, and a removable 500 GB hard drive, per Pace’s platform announcements and contemporary reporting.Lightwave Engadget

It’s a rented box, not a sold one — most XG1s in the wild were Comcast property on loan, which is one more reason this report stays in the public record. The FCC filing (ID NQ8PX001ANM) is unusually two-faced: internal photos, the user manual, and test reports are public, while the schematics, block diagram, and parts list are permanently confidential.FCC filing

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The outside

The FCC’s public external photos show a full-size set-top chassis; the user manual documents the front panel, rear connections, and CableCARD slot. This is the flagship form factor of the cable era — wide, vented, and built to live under a TV for years.FCC filing

The board

The public internal-photo exhibit shows the main board, tuner section, and hard-drive bay — but with the schematics and block diagram permanently confidential, public records name no confirmed chip IDs for this unit. We will not guess at them. Even the exhibit list names a “GP500C datasheet” among the items Pace asked the FCC to keep private.FCC filing

The FCC filing

FCC ID NQ8PX001ANM, originally filed by Pace Micro Technology / Pace Plc, with applications dated January 18, 2012. The filing describes a “High Definition Set Top Box PX001ANM” and lists a 2.405–2.4835 GHz entry at 2 mW — the low-power link for the RF remote. Internal and external photos, the user manual, and setup photos were released July 16, 2012; the Part 15B/15C reports, RF exposure statement, and labels are public. The schematics, block diagram, operational description, bill of materials, and the GP500C datasheet are permanently confidential.FCC filing

One caution: don’t confuse this unit with the XG1v3 (Pace PX013ANM, FCC ID NQ8PX013ANM) — a different generation under a different filing.FCC filing (XG1v3)

Service and debug modes

The public user manual documents setup, remote pairing (IR and RF), and on-screen reset procedures.FCC filing We found no publicly documented UART, JTAG, or SWD interface for this unit — we listen, we don’t knock.

Words to know

QAM tuner
A receiver for cable-TV signals — QAM is the modulation cable plants use. Six tuners means six channels at once.
CableCARD
A PCMCIA-sized card that decrypts premium cable channels, so the box itself doesn’t need a built-in security chip.
DOCSIS
The standard for two-way data over cable lines — how the DVR talks back to Comcast.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
