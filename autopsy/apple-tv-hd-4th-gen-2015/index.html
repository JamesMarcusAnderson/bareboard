The Autopsy Report · Device #044

The Apple TV HD (2015)

The box that brought apps to the Apple TV: an A8 chip, a touch remote, and a USB-C port Apple meant for its own technicians. We haven’t opened one — here’s the public record.

What is this thing?

The fourth-generation Apple TV — later renamed the Apple TV HD — is Apple’s 2015 streaming box and the first to run tvOS with an App Store. Inside: an Apple A8 (APL1011), 2 GB of SK hynix LPDDR3, and 32 GB of SK hynix NAND, per iFixit’s September 22, 2015 teardown.MacRumors iFixit

It kept a USB-C port on the back for diagnostics and service — the port Apple would remove from the 4K model two years later — and earned the same 8/10 repairability score as its successor.iFixit MacRumors

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The outside

A black puck in the same family silhouette as its successor, paired with the Siri Remote. The teardown photos show the back panel’s USB-C diagnostic/service connector — the visible door Apple built for its own technicians.iFixit

The board

iFixit found a larger heatsink and a modular power supply on the board, alongside the USB-C service connector. The Apple A8 (APL1011) does the computing; a 2 GB SK hynix LPDDR3 package and a 32 GB SK hynix NAND package handle memory and storage. Wireless runs through a USI 339S00045 module, associated in teardown reporting with the Broadcom BCM4345; wired networking is 10/100 through an SMSC/Microchip LAN9730 USB-to-Ethernet bridge; and an Apple-branded 338S00057 is read as a memory controller.MacRumors TechInfoDepot

The FCC filing

FCC ID BCGA1625, Apple Inc., grant dated September 9, 2015, model A1625. Test data: Bluetooth 0.02254 W, 2.4 GHz WLAN 0.359 W, and 5 GHz entries up to 0.124 W.FCC filing

Service and debug modes

The USB-C port on the back is a documented service/diagnostic port — the one public, Apple-built door into this box. We found no publicly documented UART, JTAG, or SWD interface — we listen, we don’t knock.

What the public record tells us

  • A service port you could see. Unlike its successor, this box kept USB-C diagnostics on the back panel — no prying required.iFixit
  • Wired networking came via USB. Ethernet runs through an SMSC LAN9730 USB-to-Ethernet bridge — 10/100, not gigabit — and Microchip publishes its datasheet for anyone to read.Datasheet (PDF)

Words to know

USB-to-Ethernet bridge
A chip that gives a device wired networking over its USB bus — the A8 here talks USB, and the LAN9730 translates to Ethernet.
Memory controller
The traffic cop between the processor and the memory chips — here reportedly an Apple-branded part, the 338S00057.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
