The Fire TV Stick 4K (2018)

In October 2018, Amazon put 4K, Dolby Vision, and HDR10+ into a $49.99 stick. We haven’t had one on our bench — here’s everything the public record tells us about what’s inside.

What is this thing?

The Fire TV Stick 4K is Amazon’s 2018 streaming stick: a small HDMI dongle that plugs into a TV and runs Fire OS apps — Netflix, Prime Video, and the rest — at up to 4K60 with Dolby Vision, HDR10, and HDR10+. Amazon announced it on October 3, 2018 at $49.99, with shipping expected by the end of that month.AppleInsider CNX Software

Under the hood it’s a MediaTek design: an MT8695 applications processor (quad-core ARM at up to 1.7 GHz with a PowerVR GE8300 GPU) paired with an MT7668 wireless companion chip for Wi-Fi 5 (2×2 MU-MIMO) and Bluetooth 5.0. Amazon’s developer specs list 1.5 GB of DDR4 memory and 8 GB of internal storage.CNX Software WikiDevi

Not yet on our bench

Let's be direct: BareBoard has never had this device on our bench. We have not possessed it, opened it, photographed it, measured it, or logged it. We have not captured a boot log, probed a debug port, or run a single test. Everything on this page comes from public records — FCC filings, manufacturer documentation, and published teardowns — linked inline so you can check every claim.

The things only a bench can provide — our own photos, physical measurements, a boot log, board-level confirmation of chip markings, and the exact revision of whatever unit eventually reaches us — are all still ahead of us. Until then, this is a public-records autopsy, and we say so out loud.

The outside

The stick measures 90 × 30 × 14 mm and weighs 53.6 grams — a small black dongle with an HDMI plug on one end and a micro-USB port (power only) on the side. It shipped with an Alexa Voice Remote, a power adapter, and two AAA batteries.CNX Software

The board

Public hardware records describe a two-chip MediaTek architecture: the MT8695 applications processor and the MT7668 wireless chip, with 1.5 GB of DDR4 and 8 GB of flash alongside. Amazon’s developer documentation confirms the same bill of materials.WikiDevi CNX Software Board-level photography beyond the enclosure isn’t in the public sources we reviewed — the FCC filing’s exhibits went public after short-term confidentiality expired on April 1, 2019.FCC filing

The FCC filing

FCC ID 2AQIQ-6247, grantee MX Processing LLC, grant dated October 3, 2018. The filing describes an “HDMI Digital Media Receiver,” model E9L29Y. Test data lists 2.4 GHz WLAN conducted power up to 0.5224 W combined, Bluetooth at 0.004 W, and 5 GHz entries up to 0.1892 W across 20/40/80 MHz channels with 2×2 MIMO.FCC filing

Service and debug modes

Consumer-facing only: the Alexa Voice Remote pairs over Bluetooth, and the quick-start guide in the FCC filing documents setup.CNX Software FCC filing We found no publicly documented UART, JTAG, or SWD interface for this device — we listen, we don’t knock, and here the public record shows no door.

What the public record tells us

  • The two-chip split. Compute on the MT8695, radio on the MT7668 — a clean division that the public records agree on.WikiDevi
  • $49.99 for 4K. At launch the 4K stick cost only $10 more than Amazon’s 1080p stick — Dolby Vision in the cheapest seat in the house.CNX Software

Words to know

MU-MIMO
Multi-user MIMO — lets one Wi-Fi radio talk to several devices at once instead of queuing them up.
Dolby Vision / HDR10 / HDR10+
High-dynamic-range video standards — brighter highlights, deeper shadows. Dolby Vision carries scene-by-scene brightness instructions; HDR10+ is the royalty-free answer to it.
HDCP 2.2
The copy-protection handshake 4K content requires over HDMI.
System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and I/O — on one piece of silicon.
FCC
The Federal Communications Commission — the U.S. agency that regulates anything that transmits radio. Every wireless gadget sold here has to pass its tests first.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. The first few characters are the company's grantee code; the rest is the maker's model code. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Teardown
A published disassembly of a device, usually with photos and chip identification — someone else's bench work we can learn from.
UART / serial console
A simple port that lets you watch a device narrate its own startup, line by line. We listen; we don't knock.
JTAG / SWD
Deep debug ports wired straight into the chip — powerful, and easy to break things with. We map them from public records and leave them alone.
Firmware
The software baked into the device — the part that runs before anything else.
eMMC / NAND flash
The chip that holds the device's software when the power is off.
LPDDR
Low-power DDR memory — the short-term workspace chips in phones, tablets, and streamers.
