The original iPad (2010)

The one that started it all: Apple's A4, a 9.7-inch screen, and a board full of first-generation choices.

What is this thing?

The first-generation iPad is the tablet Apple announced on January 27, 2010 and released on April 3, 2010, starting at $499. Wikipedia It created the modern tablet category more or less single-handedly: a 9.7-inch multitouch screen running a stretched-out iPhone OS.

Inside, it's a milestone twice over: the Apple A4 was Apple's first own-designed system-on-a-chip, and the board around it is a snapshot of Apple's 2010 supply chain — Samsung and Toshiba flash, Broadcom radios and touch controllers. MacRumors EE Times

Not yet on our bench

This device is not on our bench. We don't own one, we haven't opened one, and we haven't powered one on. Everything on this page comes from public records: government filings, published teardowns by repair shops and journalists, and manufacturer documents. That's a legitimate way to learn a device's story — and it's also a limited one. There are no BareBoard photos, measurements, boot logs, or firmware notes here, because we haven't earned them yet.

When a unit of the original iPad does reach our bench, this page gets a second edition: our own board photography, our own measurements, and whatever the device tells us when it wakes up. Until then, what follows is the public record, carefully sourced, with the gaps labeled as gaps.

The outside

A 9.7-inch, 1024×768 multitouch display in an aluminum unibody with a thick black bezel by modern standards. Wikipedia One home button, a 30-pin dock connector on the bottom edge, and — on the 3G model — a black plastic window in the back for the cellular antenna.

The stock photo below shows the original iPad next to its much smaller 2012 sibling: same design language, very different footprint.

The board

Contemporary reporting on the FCC's internal photos describes a single logic board with the A4 at its center, Broadcom radio and touch chips nearby, and Toshiba (or Samsung, depending on the unit) NAND flash providing storage. AppleInsider MacRumors

iFixit's archived A4 teardown walks the board chip by chip, confirming 256 MB of RAM stacked with the processor and the Broadcom touch-controller pair. iFixit (archived PDF) We have not photographed the board ourselves.

Debug and service modes

Apple publicly documents recovery mode for iPads that won't update or restore — the support article walks through connecting to a computer and restoring the device. Apple Support Below that sits DFU mode, a deeper restore state described in public repair references.

We have not put any iPad into either mode. They are listed here because they are publicly documented — not because we've used them.

Our posture on all of the above: we describe what's publicly documented, and we don't go knocking. No device has been probed, reflashed, or opened on our bench — there is nothing here but reading.

What public records show

The A4 was the real story. Before the iPad, Apple bought its mobile chips; the A4 — designed in-house after the P.A. Semi acquisition — made Apple a chip company. Engadget's 2010 investigation walks through how unusual that was. Engadget

Broadcom owned the radios and the touch. With the Wi-Fi/BT combo and both touch controllers, Broadcom was the biggest third-party winner on the board — a position it would hold across several iPad generations. EE Times

Words to know

Every term this page uses, in plain English. No prior knowledge required.

System on a chip (SoC)
The whole computer — processor, graphics, and memory interfaces — on one piece of silicon. The A4 was Apple's first.
NAND flash
The chip that holds the operating system and user data when the power is off.
Touchscreen controller
The chip that turns finger touches on the glass into coordinates the processor understands.
FCC ID
The ID the FCC assigns to each approved wireless product. Punch it into fccid.io and the whole public filing comes up.
Recovery mode
A state Apple documents for restoring an iPad whose software won't boot — it reloads the OS from a computer.
DFU mode
Device Firmware Update: an even deeper restore state that talks to the bootloader before iOS loads.
