Autopsy Report: Device #029 · Technics SL-1200 Turntable Line (1972–Present)

What is this thing?

The Technics SL-1200 is a direct-drive high-torque analogue playback machine. It is an industrial electromechanical engineering milestone that uses zero digital code blocks, network tracking paths, or operating software to rotate and track vinyl media at exact speeds.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

The mechanical design exposes high-precision adjustment points directly on the surface boundaries, enabling operators to fine-tune, monitor, and maintain speed parameters without touching internal circuitry:

  • The Stroboscopic Calibration Grid: 3 The dots engraved into the edge of the turntable are a symbol of the SL-1200 Series. When illuminated by the integrated red neon/LED guide light on the chassis panel while spinning, the dots appear to freeze if the platter hits exactly 33⅓ or 45 RPM. This allows users to physically audit speed drift down to fractional percentages entirely by sight.
    Correction: The original text said "33.3 RPM." The correct notation is 33⅓ RPM (33 and one-third).
  • The External Tonearm Rigidity Controls: The base of the gimbal tonearm assembly houses integrated anti-skating adjustment knobs and a threaded helical height ring. These allow users to change tracking force parameters on the fly using mechanical linkages, ensuring precision pickup stabilization without altering internal assemblies.

History & Context

Correction – Manufacturer attribution: The original text says "Launched by Panasonic." 4 The SL-1200 series was launched by Matsushita Electric under the Technics brand. While Panasonic is the modern successor brand of Matsushita, the historically accurate attribution for the 1972 launch is Technics (Matsushita Electric Industrial Co.).
Correction – Units sold: The original text says "surpassing 3.5 million units." 5 6 Multiple sources (including Technics' own 50th anniversary materials and Wikipedia) state that over 3 million SL-1200 units were sold — not 3.5 million. The 3.5 million figure appears to be inflated.
Correction – Motor type: The original text says "coreless direct-drive motors." The SL-1200 uses a brushless DC direct-drive motor. "Coreless" is not the standard description; the key innovation was the brushless direct-drive system.
Addition – Production hiatus: 4 5 Production of the SL-1200 was discontinued in 2010 and the line was relaunched in 2016 with the Grand Class SL-1200G. This hiatus is a significant part of the product's history.

Addition – Production hiatus: 4 The SL-1200 series remained in production from 1972 to 2010, and then again from 2016 to the present. The original text's date range of "1972–Present" is technically misleading without noting the six-year discontinuation.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched under the Technics brand by Matsushita Electric in 1972 and surpassing 3 million units sold, the SL-1200 is a monument to industrial repairability. 5 The Technics SL-1200 turntable and its many iterations have been in production for 43 of the last 49 years — the exception being a hiatus between 2010 and 2016 when Matsushita/Panasonic suspended the Technics brand. By ditching failure-prone rubber drive belts for a quartz-locked direct-drive motor, it eliminated structural wear points. 8 It is widely regarded as one of the most durable and reliable turntables ever produced. Many 1970s units are still in heavy use.

Autopsy Report: Device #030 · Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses

What is this thing?

The Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses represent an ultra-dense wearable computer built into an eyewear frame. It combines high-resolution imaging, directional open-ear audio tracking, and wireless communication chips to process context locally while passing sensor telemetry directly to an associated network device.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

Because the compact frame is entirely sealed against environmental moisture and uses rigid resin to pack components, diagnostics rely entirely on using its built-in external data contacts and physical indicator logic:

  • The Pogo-Pin Bridge Handshake: Tucked inside the hinge mechanism or the custom charging case panel sit gold-plated pogo-pin data contacts. Connecting the glasses to a diagnostic terminal over this interface bridges the internal system controller's USB bus line, allowing software engineers to pull error metrics, battery monitoring logs, and internal thermal states without damaging the frame
  • The Capture LED Hardware Interlock: 13 The small LED mounted next to the camera lens is physically hardwired into the camera power circuit. The camera cannot be energized without simultaneously illuminating the LED, preventing hidden software-layer activation without physical warning.
    Note on controversy: The claim that this LED is truly "un-skippable" has been a subject of criticism and security research. While it is hardware-interlocked, the LED's small size has been criticized as being too easy to miss or conceal physically.

History & Context

Correction – Units sold: The original text claims "crossing over 2 million units deployed." This significantly understates the real figures. 18 EssilorLuxottica reported that AI-glasses unit sales developed with Meta exceeded seven million in 2025. The 2 million figure may have been accurate at an earlier point but is now outdated.
Correction – Weight: The original text says "standard 50-gram acetate frame." According to Ray-Ban's own specifications, 14 the glasses weigh 133 g, which is far from 50 grams. The 50g figure is incorrect and should be removed.
Correction – Microphone count: The original text says "dual-microphone arrays." The actual product uses a five-microphone array, as confirmed by 15 five microphones listed in the specs. The Gen 3 model uses 18 six microphones.
Addition – Water resistance: 10 Meta also finally added IPX4 water resistance to the Ray-Ban Meta glasses.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in late 2023 (expanding heavily through 2024/2025) and exceeding 7 million units sold by end of 2025, this wearable platform 10 is built on Qualcomm's new AR1 Gen 1 platform. It demonstrates extreme spatial compression, packaging a full wearable-class processor, a five-microphone array, and Wi-Fi basebands into a 133-gram acetate frame with IPX4 water resistance. It serves as a prime study node for high-density mobile power allocation.

Autopsy Report: Device #031 · Whisker Litter-Robot Line (2000–Present)

What is this thing?

The Litter-Robot is an automated, heavy-duty electromechanical appliance built around a rotating drum design. It utilizes pressure switches, optical sensors, and high-torque motor gearboxes to handle mechanical filtration tasks entirely locally on an automated tracking loop.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

Unlike conventional networked devices, the Litter-Robot's local mechanical systems can be tested directly through physical button sequences and external mechanical adjustments:

  • The Control Panel Key Overrides: By running specific button sequences on the front panel interface (such as holding down the Cycle and Empty nodes simultaneously), a user can force the internal controller to cycle through a built-in diagnostic test sequence. The system flashes its status LEDs to signal if the weight sensors are uncalibrated or if the safety pinch-sensor contacts have oxidized.
    Correction: The original text mentions "laser weight pads." The Litter-Robot 4 uses 26 patented machine-learning, laser and weight sensor technologies, but this is specific to the LR4 and newer units. Earlier models used simpler cat-detection mechanisms. Calling them "laser weight pads" conflates two separate sensor systems.
  • The Cat-Detect Weight Calibration: The lower frame cavity mounts an adjustable internal micro-switch linked to a physical spring weight pad. Users can tune the sensitivity threshold externally by turning a simple plastic thumb screw located under the base shell. This alters the mechanical spring tension directly to re-calibrate the cat-detection safety loop without opening the control board box.

History & Context

Correction – Units sold: The original text says "over 2 million units in active service." 20 The most verifiable figure is that Litter-Robot surpassed 1 million units sold as of 2023. The 2 million figure is not supported by the available sources and appears to be inflated.
Correction – Wi-Fi connectivity timeline: The original text implies Wi-Fi was present from early models. 19 In fact, Whisker introduced its first WiFi-enabled unit (Litter-Robot 3 Connect) in 2017. Earlier models had no wireless connectivity.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in 2000 and reaching over 1 million units sold as of 2023, this platform shows the scale of automated home robotics. 20 In 2017, the company released its first WiFi-enabled unit, Litter-Robot 3 Connect. While modern iterations include Wi-Fi logging features and laser/weight sensor technologies, the fundamental mechanical skeleton relies on simple, high-torque DC motors and basic microchip limit switches. Its modular mechanical setup allows it to remain functional for years through simple part swapping.

Autopsy Report: Device #032 · HP DeskJet Line (1988–Present)

What is this thing?

The HP DeskJet line is a foundational inkjet printing platform that democratized high-quality home document production. It uses thermal inkjet micro-nozzles to vaporize ink droplets onto paper at high speed, forming the basis for modern consumer printing.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

Despite being a paper-handling peripheral, the DeskJet exposes several key diagnostic interfaces that allow technicians to test the print engine without a computer connection:

  • The Power-On Self-Test Page: Holding down the power button while pressing the resume key forces the printer to run an internal self-test. The machine prints a diagnostic page showing ink nozzle health, firmware revision, and page count — all without any USB or network data link.
  • The Cartridge Alignment Bypass: Removing the ink cartridges and closing the lid triggers the printer's internal optical sensor to run a carriage-stall test. This exposes whether the belt drive or encoder strip has degraded, using only the front-panel blinking light codes.

History & Context

Verified – Launch and significance: 31 29 The claim that the DeskJet "democratized" home printing is well-supported. The original HP DeskJet (1988) was the first mass-market inkjet printer, and the line has sold hundreds of millions of units.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in 1988 with the original HP DeskJet, this line pioneered affordable thermal inkjet printing for the home. 29 With hundreds of millions of units sold across its many generations, it remains one of the most successful printer lines in history. Its simple, robust paper-path mechanics and user-replaceable ink cartridges made it a model of accessible repairability.

Autopsy Report: Device #033 · Apple iPhone 6 / 6 Plus (2014–2016)

What is this thing?

The iPhone 6 and 6 Plus represent Apple's transition to large-format smartphones. Built around the Apple A8 System-on-a-Chip, they introduced a 4.7-inch and 5.5-inch display to Apple's lineup, along with NFC-based Apple Pay and a thinner unibody aluminum design.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

As sealed devices, the iPhone 6 exposes limited external diagnostics, but several non-invasive test points remain:

  • The Field Test Mode: Dialing *3001#12345#* opens the iOS Field Test menu, exposing raw cellular signal metrics (RSRP, RSRQ), carrier aggregation status, and baseband firmware version — all without jailbreaking or opening the device.
  • The Lightning Port Diagnostics: The Lightning connector exposes USB data lines that, with the right adapter, allow technicians to pull crash logs, battery cycle counts, and thermal sensor data via Apple's internal diagnostic protocols.

History & Context

Verified – Sales record: 44 The claim of 10 million units sold in the first three days is correct and well-documented. This was a record at the time.
Verified – Total sales: 46 40 The iPhone 6/6 Plus sold approximately 222 million units, making it one of the best-selling smartphones of all time.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in September 2014 and selling 10 million units in its first weekend, the iPhone 6 generation moved approximately 222 million units globally. 44 46 Powered by the Apple A8 chip and introducing Apple Pay via NFC, it marked Apple's decisive move into the phablet era. Its unibody aluminum design, however, introduced the "Bendgate" structural controversy, which Apple addressed with stronger 7000-series aluminum in the 6s.

Autopsy Report: Device #034 · Nokia 105 Line (2013–Present)

What is this thing?

The Nokia 105 is an ultra-basic candybar feature phone built for maximum battery life and durability. It runs Nokia's Series 30+ operating system on a highly integrated single-chip baseband processor, with no app store, no touchscreen, and no internet browser in its earliest versions.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

As a simple device, the Nokia 105 exposes its full diagnostic interface through keypad codes:

  • The Global Firmware Variant Call: Typing the sequence *#0000# commands the internal operating system to display its exact factory firmware compilation date, software version number, and baseband layout tags. This allows users to verify their configuration variant without any local computer data links.

History & Context

Launched in 2013 and passing 200 million units in active rotation globally, the Nokia 105 line acts as the baseline communications network anchor for massive developing regions. Built around a highly consolidated MediaTek / Intel Mobile platform running Nokia's Series 30+ operating software, it contains zero data-mining tracking loops or always-on internet pipelines, making it an exceptional case study in pure, low-power cellular utility.

Correction – Processor: The original text says "MediaTek / Intel Mobile platform." The Nokia 105 (2013) uses the MediaTek MT6260A. Intel was never used in the Nokia 105 line; this appears to be a confusion with other Nokia models.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in 2013 and exceeding 200 million units across its generations, the Nokia 105 is built on the MediaTek MT6260A single-chip platform running Series 30+. Its weeks-long standby battery life and nearly indestructible candybar form factor made it the default communications tool across developing markets.

Autopsy Report: Device #035 · HP LaserJet Line (1984–Present)

What is this thing?

The HP LaserJet line is a heavy-duty, high-volume document rendering infrastructure framework. It utilizes static high-voltage internal drums, laser optical mirrors, and intense thermal heat fuser elements to bond carbon toner particles straight to physical paper sheets.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

Unlike modern consumer inkjet printing setups that are crippled by software paywalls and network lockouts, standard business-class LaserJet systems can be diagnosed and maintained entirely from the physical front panel controls:

  • The Built-In Engine Test Bypass: On almost all LaserJet units, a tiny, recessed mechanical micro-switch is positioned on the exterior side panel housing. Inserting a non-metallic probe straight into this hole flips the internal logic gates. The printer automatically bypasses the main formatting board, the network card, and all computer print queues to print a series of engine test pages.
  • The Control Panel Event Log: Navigating the front-panel menu to the diagnostics section exposes the internal event log, showing fuser temperature errors, paper-jam counters, and formatter board faults — all retrievable without opening the chassis.

History & Context

Correction – Units sold: The original text claims "crossing 200 million units deployed." 34 This figure is unverifiable for the LaserJet line specifically. HP has sold hundreds of millions of printers overall, but the 200 million figure appears to conflate LaserJet with DeskJet sales. No source confirms 200 million LaserJets.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in 1984 with the original HP LaserJet (built on Canon's CX engine), this line defined business laser printing. 34 While exact cumulative sales are not publicly confirmed, the LaserJet remains the dominant office printer platform. Its modular toner-cartridge design and accessible paper path made it exceptionally serviceable.

Autopsy Report: Device #036 · Apple iPhone 6s / 6s Plus (2015–2018)

What is this thing?

The iPhone 6s and 6s Plus refined the large-format iPhone with a stronger 7000-series aluminum unibody, 3D Touch pressure-sensitive display, and the Apple A9 System-on-a-Chip with 2GB of LPDDR4 RAM.

Non-Destructive Interfacing

  • The 3D Touch Calibration: The pressure-sensitive display can be tested through Settings > General > Accessibility, where users can adjust and verify the force-touch sensitivity thresholds without any hardware disassembly.
  • The NVMe Storage Check: The 6s was Apple's first iPhone with NVMe-based flash storage. Third-party tools can query the Lightning port to report NAND wear levels and read/write speeds, exposing storage health non-invasively.

History & Context

Correction – Units sold: The original text says "crossing 174.5 million units sold globally." 42 The verifiable figure is approximately 174 million. The 174.5 million figure implies a false precision; sources cite ~174 million.
Correction – Fabrication nodes: The original text says "dual-sourced across Samsung's 14nm and TSMC's 16nm fabrication nodes." This is correct and well-documented.
Verified – M9 co-processor integration: The claim that Apple integrated the M9 motion co-processor directly into the A9 die is correct.

Corrected paragraph:

Launched in September 2015 and crossing approximately 174 million units sold globally, the 6s generation corrected the structural flaws of its predecessor by using 7000-series aluminum alloy (zinc-alloyed). Powered by the Apple A9 System-on-a-Chip (dual-sourced across Samsung's 14nm and TSMC's 16nm fabrication nodes) paired with 2GB of LPDDR4 memory, this chip marks the transition where Apple integrated the M9 Motion Co-processor directly into the main silicon die, showing how separate tracking loops get folded into a single central chip core over time.
